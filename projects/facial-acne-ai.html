Problem

General-purpose LLMs can answer skincare questions, but their responses are often ungrounded — no citations, no tie to clinical literature, and no ability to analyze what they actually see in a photo. For acne specifically, users need answers that combine visual observation with evidence from dermatology research.

Facial Acne AI addresses this with a local multimodal pipeline: a vision-language model structures observations from face photos, a RAG system retrieves relevant paper excerpts, and the same model synthesizes a grounded answer with numbered citations.

Research Corpus

Dermatology papers are converted to markdown (via Marker PDF pipeline), chunked, and ingested into ChromaDB. The corpus covers five topic areas:

  • Hormones & genetics

    Puberty, genetics, and internal body changes that cause acne.

  • Bacteria & inflammation

    Skin bacteria (e.g. C. acnes) and inflammatory responses.

  • Environment & stress

    Sweat, products, towels, and mental stress effects on skin.

  • Medicine & skincare

    Retinoids, benzoyl peroxide, azelaic acid, and proven ingredients.

  • Diet & habits

    Food, sleep, and washing habits in relation to skin health.

Human Evaluation

A blinded human evaluation compared base Qwen2.5-VL responses against RAG-augmented responses across 6 paired prompts (5 text + 1 photo query), scored by 5 independent reviewers on groundedness, completeness, and safety (1–5 scale).

Condition Groundedness Completeness Safety
Base (no RAG) 2.5 4.2 4.0
RAG 4.6 3.8 4.7

RAG was preferred by reviewer majority on 5 of 6 prompts. All 28 inline citation claims in RAG responses were manually verified as fully supported by retrieved excerpts (100% citation accuracy).
