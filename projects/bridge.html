Problem

LLMs are increasingly deployed to audit institutional text — housing ordinances, legal contracts, government reports — for discriminatory language. Yet nearly all bias benchmarks (BBQ, StereoSet, CrowS-Pairs, ToxiGen, Social Bias Frames) are built from informal, social-media-style text.

This creates a structural mismatch: models evaluated on tweets are deployed on statutes. We discovered that this mismatch produces a systematic failure mode called the Register Polarity Flip (RPF) — models under-detect harmful bias in formal text while over-flagging neutral informal text as harmful, with consistent directionality across all eight frontier models tested.

The BRIDGE Benchmark

BRIDGE (Bias Register-Integrated Dataset for Generalized Evaluation) is the first public benchmark unifying formal institutional and informal social text under a shared three-way taxonomy, assembled from seven open-source corpora spanning 150 years of US discourse.

GRDC Diagnostic Suite

We designed GRDC — a five-metric framework for group-conditional bias evaluation that generalizes to any categorical grouping variable (register, era, source, domain):

  • Grouped Recall Divergence (GRD)

    Measures magnitude of recall disparity between groups. Global mean GRD = 0.271 across eight models.

  • Directional Consistency (DC)

    Tests whether all models shift recall in the same direction. DC = 1.0 for both harmful and harmless classes simultaneously.

  • Register Polarity Flip (RPF)

    Detects when models over-detect bias in one register while under-detecting in another — the core failure mode.

  • Confusion Drift (CD)

    Localizes which misclassification cells change across register groups.

  • Capability-Conditional Drift (CCD)

    Correlates model capability (macro-F1) with register divergence — stronger models may exhibit larger formal-register blindness.

Key Findings

  • Register Polarity Flip is universal

    All eight frontier LLMs simultaneously miss harmful formal text (~45 recall points below informal) and over-flag neutral informal text (~30 recall points above formal), with DC = 1.0 (p ≈ 6×10⁻⁵).

  • Replication on external data

    RPF replicates on an independent dataset (BABE + Measuring Hate Speech) with a larger effect (GRD = 0.504 vs. 0.271 on BRIDGE).

  • Few-shot prompting does not fix it

    Register-stratified failures persist under few-shot and zero-shot prompting across racial, gender, and political bias dimensions.

  • Model rankings shift with register mix

    Sensitivity analysis shows model leaderboards change at just 10% formal text composition — aggregate accuracy masks deployment-relevant failures.
