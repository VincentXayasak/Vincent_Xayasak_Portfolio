Problem

Reasoning models are compared almost entirely by a single number — Pass@1 accuracy on a benchmark. But this scalar is a lossy projection: two models tied on Pass@1 can behave entirely differently underneath.

On a law-reasoning benchmark, Claude-4.5-Sonnet and Gemini-2.5-Pro differ by less than half a percentage point on Pass@1 — yet their reasoning-path consistency differs by 0.17 on a 0–1 scale. On mathematics, GPT-OSS-120B and GPT-5-mini are likewise tied on Pass@1, but by the fourth attempt their accuracies differ by more than ten points. The scalar says they are equal; the behavior says they are not.

Capability is not a scalar — it has a shape. We introduce two complementary instruments to measure that shape.

Reasoning Path Consistency (RPC)

Given a model's four reasoning traces on the same problem, RPC measures how similar those paths are across three complementary axes:

  • RPC-L (lexical)

    Token n-gram overlap (n = 3) capturing surface-level phrasing reuse across attempts.

  • RPC-S (structural)

    Similarity of step-type sequences (reasoning, tool call, observation) — whether the model organizes its solution the same way.

  • RPC-Sem (semantic)

    Mean pairwise cosine similarity of sentence embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2). Primary axis, validated against human judgments (Spearman ρ = 0.34, n = 50, p = 0.017).

Parametric Sampling Fingerprints (PSF)

PSF fits a three-parameter saturation curve to each model's empirical Pass@k trajectory (k = 1…4):

Pass@k = α + (β − α) · (1 − e−γ(k−1))
Parameter Meaning
α First-attempt accuracy (Pass@1)
β Saturation ceiling — maximum accuracy approached as k grows
γ Approach rate — how quickly additional samples convert to correct answers

The triple (α, β, γ) is a compact fingerprint of sampling behavior, making two models directly comparable even when their Pass@1 is identical. Mean R² = 0.96 across non-degenerate fits.

Key Findings

  • Pass@1-matched models diverge in reasoning consistency

    Semantic RPC gaps up to 0.17 between models with nearly identical first-attempt accuracy — overwhelmingly statistically significant.

  • Pass@1-matched models diverge in sampling behavior

    Pass@4 gaps up to 10 percentage points — one model saturates immediately while another keeps improving with more samples.

  • RPC and PSF are statistically independent

    |r| ≤ 0.14 between PSF parameters and RPC axes — reasoning consistency and sampling trajectory capture distinct dimensions, not two views of the same signal.

  • Behavior shape is domain-conditional

    A model's fingerprint changes across reasoning domains, most strongly in structural organization of its reasoning paths.

Analysis Pipeline

I built a Python analysis pipeline (squadc CLI) that processes multi-attempt benchmark outputs end-to-end:

  • Data cleaning & verification

    Streaming dataloader and validation scripts for AGIEval, AIME, GPQA, and MMMU benchmark outputs with attempt-level correctness extraction.

  • RPC computation

    Separate modules for lexical n-gram overlap (RPC-L), structural step-sequence similarity via LCS (RPC-S), and semantic embedding cosine similarity (RPC-Sem).

  • PSF fitting

    Pass@k estimation, three-parameter curve fitting with bootstrap confidence intervals, and matched-Pass@1 pair analysis with distribution artifacts.

  • Cross-analysis & experiments

    PSF clustering in (α, β, γ) space, RPC–PSF independence checks, and five reproducibility experiments (R1–R5) with CDF plots and paper-ready summaries.
